One coherent path

From microscopic model to physical claim

The core algorithm owns block diagonalization. The utility layer owns physical construction, reconstruction, and diagnostics. Keeping this boundary explicit prevents convenience functions from silently making physical choices.

Nathan–Rudner universal Lindblad equation

A physical front end, not a competing reducer

The universal Lindblad equation closes a microscopic system–bath model into a full-system GKSL generator. Pymablock can then reduce that generator. The two approximations act at different levels and carry separate error budgets.

microscopic closure

The bath spectrum and coupling operators determine explicit jumps and a Lamb shift without a rotating-wave condition on system level spacings.

factor first

For H = H₀ + λV, expand each jump through nested commutators or a Dyson series, then construct its dissipator.

dressed state

The derivation evolves a density matrix perturbatively close to the physical reduced state. That dressing belongs in the approximation metadata.

secondary reduction

𝓛ULE is a physical Pymablock input, but eliminating another fast sector can still produce a non-GKSL effective block.

This is also a constructive version of the jump-series problem. Keeping the truncated jump inside its full dissipator is pointwise completely positive but retains terms beyond the requested order. Re-expanding and truncating the dissipator returns to the finite-order Kossakowski test used below. See Nathan and Rudner.

Interpretation is an output

One report, four physical statuses

A non-GKSL coefficient is not a sufficient diagnosis. The report must say whether the defect is physical at the retained order and whether the model remains valid on the embedded slow manifold.

report = analyze_lindbladian(
  L_eff,
  embedding=K,
  order=4,
)

report.trace_preserving = True
report.hermiticity_preserving = True
report.cp_status = "formal_obstruction"
report.witness = σz/√2
report.intertwining_residual = 8.7e−15

gksl

indeterminate_at_order

formal_obstruction

Negativity appears at its first nonzero order; higher orders cannot repair it near the expansion point.

embedded_only

The generator accurately evolves the physical slow manifold but is not a channel on every abstract reduced state.

Concrete validation targets

Applications that exercise different layers

These examples are not decoration. Together they test construction, odd and even orders, embeddings, positivity diagnostics, reduced bases, and dressed response.

Thermal oscillator + qubit

Tokieda: detect a genuine CP obstruction

Eliminate a damped oscillator through fourth order, reconstruct the Kossakowski series, and find the negative dephasing direction inside ker(C₂).

The embedding remains accurate even though the reduced generator cannot act as an autonomous channel on arbitrary qubit states.

Observed: ⟨Fz, C₄Fz⟩ = −11.299 · residual 8.7×10⁻¹⁵

Electron-mediated nuclear decay

Kessler: recognize an incomplete square

The second-order collective jump is dressed at third order by a polarization-dependent phase. The cross term alone is not manifestly GKSL.

Resumming the jump supplies a positive fourth-order square, so any apparent CP defect lies beyond third-order accuracy.

Observed: third-order coefficient agreement 4.6×10⁻¹⁶

Steady-state parameter sweeps

Melo: export one embedding column as a reduced basis

Convert Pymablock’s stationary embedding to the Moore–Penrose normalization gauge, orthonormalize its coefficients, and reuse their span with variational weights.

This adds a post-processing layer without changing block diagonalization.

Observed: gauge-converted series agreement 2.4×10⁻¹⁶

Minimal Kitaev junction readout

Kuhn et al.: dress the qubit and its readout channels

Use Hermitian Pymablock to eliminate high-energy junction sectors, then transform gate, current, and bath-coupling operators with the same series.

A small resolvent helper can evaluate Hamiltonian, Sisyphus, and Hermes susceptibility contributions without pretending linear response is block PT.

Target: effective qubit + dressed jumps + finite-frequency susceptibility

Two reservoirs + dense many-body spectrum

Nathan–Rudner: construct before reducing

Build the ULE for a Heisenberg chain coupled at opposite ends to Ohmic baths at different temperatures. The bath model produces a compact, nonsecular GKSL generator without resolving exponentially small level spacings.

A smaller chain can validate spectral-factor, Lamb-shift, and error-budget utilities. Pymablock becomes relevant only when a further low-energy or slow-mode reduction is requested.

Target: microscopic bath → guaranteed-GKSL input → optional effective block

Keep physical choices visible

What the utility layer should not automate

Several operations look mechanical but encode assumptions about timescales, environments, or reduced-state coordinates.

No automatic Markov closure

Secularization, Bloch–Redfield, and the ULE are upstream physical approximations. Pymablock accepts their generators as input but does not select or construct them.

No silent CP repair

Projecting a Kossakowski matrix onto the positive cone changes the perturbative model and can erase a genuine obstruction.

No automatic gauge choice

Partial trace, Moore–Penrose normalization, and Pymablock’s similarity gauge describe different reduced coordinates.

No universal mask

Trace, Hermiticity, and unitary lifts constrain masks differently; resonant entries belong in the retained block.

No jumps without feasibility

A trace- and Hermiticity-preserving generator always yields a Hermitian Kossakowski matrix, but physical jumps require positivity.

No hidden domain claim

A non-GKSL effective model may remain accurate on the embedded manifold. The report must preserve that distinction.
